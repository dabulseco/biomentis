2025 vs 2026

From a ten-person hackathon to a single prompt

In August 2025, the AI as a Research Assistant hackathon team set out to test whether LLMs and agentic workflows could meaningfully assist a CHIKV/Mayaro nanobody design pipeline. Their own report is the source for everything on the left side of this page. Everything on the right is drawn from the real Biomentis traces on the agent capability page.

Figure 1 from the 2025 report: ChatGPT's Agent Mode declined to engage with the team's epitope/nanobody research question.

The same class of question, two different outcomes

The 2025 team's own report documents ChatGPT's Agent Mode refusing to answer a direct question about CHIKV E1/E2 epitope consensus sequences, flagged as a possible usage-policy violation. That pushed the team toward stitching together local LLMs (Ollama, LM Studio, LLaMA 3.2), NotebookLM, and several standalone bioinformatics services by hand.

The Biomentis session on the agent capability page was asked an equivalent (arguably harder) question — design and execute a full CHIKV nanobody discovery pipeline — directly, in one conversation, and completed it.

What the team planned to automate

Reproduced from AI_vs_Traditional_Workflow.xlsx, the team's own task-by-task comparison of the traditional workflow against their proposed AI-enabled version — written before they ran it.

Activity topicTraditional workflowProposed AI-enabled workflow
Literature Review & Data CompilationManual PubMed/GenBank/PDB/IEDB searches; collection of sequences, structures, known epitopes, antibody dataRAG-enhanced LLMs auto-retrieve sequences, PDBs, epitope/antibody data; agentic scripts continuously update project databases
Sequence & Structure AnalysisManual multiple sequence alignments (ClustalOmega, MAFFT); phylogenetics; homology modeling; mapping conserved regionsAgents run alignments, phylogeny, and modeling pipelines; LLM synthesizes conservation maps, surface accessibility, and structural features
Epitope PredictionUse of BepiPred, DiscoTope, NetMHCpan, IEDB tools; predictions filtered manuallyLLM suggests input parameters; agents run predictions; AI ranks and filters epitopes for conservation, immunogenicity, accessibility
Nanobody Epitope Target SelectionBased on literature on neutralizing antibodies (E2 domains, E1 fusion loop) and structural conservationAI-guided identification of cryptic/conserved epitopes (e.g., E1 N100, E2 180–223); LLM cross-references with antibody databases
De Novo Binder / Antibody DesignIterative protein engineering guided by structural biologists; manual grafting/mutagenesisRFdiffusion for VHH backbone design; ProteinMPNN for CDR sequence generation; RoseTTAFold2 fine-tuned for antibody–antigen complexes; ParaSurf for paratope mapping
Experimental Epitope MappingPeptide arrays, alanine scanning, HDX-MS, cryo-EM, phage/yeast display; neutralization assaysAI assists in experiment prioritization and design (peptide libraries, alanine sites); agent parses assay outputs; LLM interprets structural/biophysical data
Paratope CharacterizationAntibody sequencing (VH/VL), CDR annotation, Fab crystal structures; structural superpositionLLM + agents identify germline usage, CDR motifs, electrostatic complementarity; ML/graph neural networks identify “consensus paratope” features
Nanobody Production & ScreeningExpression in E. coli/yeast; SPR/BLI affinity testing; cryo-EM confirmationAI prioritizes variants for synthesis; automated pipelines parse SPR/BLI data; AI-guided analysis of cryo-EM conformational changes
Functional ValidationIn vitro neutralization assays (PRNT, FRNT); in vivo animal protection studies; iterative antibody engineeringLLM interprets neutralization/animal model data; AI proposes multivalent/Fc-fused variants; agent re-runs in silico docking & dynamics before re-testing
Immunogen & Therapeutic DesignEpitope-focused vaccines (VLP, subunit, mRNA); bispecific antibody engineering; iterative preclinical testingLLM generative design of immunogens/antibodies; agent docking and MD simulations; AI refines candidates iteratively with minimal human intervention

2026, one Biomentis session

What actually happened this time, phase by phase

Mapped to the same phases above, using only what's verifiable from the real trace files. Where the 2025 team hit a wall, that's noted too — this isn't a claim that Biomentis reached the bench.

Phase2025 (10-person team, 4 days)2026 (single Biomentis session)
Sequence & structure processing Manual FASTA retrieval and alignment (Muscle) Handled autonomously inside the pipeline run, via query_pdb / query_uniprot tool calls — part of a single 85-step trace
Epitope identification & target selection Partial IEDB automation attempted; largely literature- and human-driven Agent named specific conserved epitope patches (Domain A central 120–149, Domain B arch 2 195–219, Domain A lateral 60–99) with conservation percentages
CDR identification Planned manually, then skipped entirely Generated and reported for all 3 candidates, with a stated design rationale per CDR
De novo binder design 3D models proposed via RFdiffusion/RFantibody; used only to guide a manually-built scaffold 3 full-length VHH sequences produced directly, ready to order as gBlocks
Affinity & cross-reactivity screening Not reached ΔG reported per candidate, per genotype (ECSA/Asian/West African), with a cross-reactivity standard deviation
Candidate selection Not formalized into a scoring system 5-criterion weighted scoring matrix, fully computed for all 3 candidates
Experimental validation Skipped — time/resource constraints Not run — this remains a wet-lab step for both years

The 2025 team's own conclusion

"Our findings suggest that human intervention is still required at key decision and validation steps, so we propose to always include human-in-the-loop approaches for AI-augmented research assistants using agents… Future work should focus on improving tool interoperability, refining prompt templates for scientific tasks, and collaborating with wet-lab researchers to validate outputs experimentally."
2025 hackathon report, Conclusion

That's still true of Biomentis: its nanobody candidates are in-silico proposals, not validated binders. What changed is where the human-in-the-loop is needed — not stitching together eight tools by hand and hoping the handoffs line up, but reviewing a finished, traceable proposal before it goes to the bench.

Figure 2 from the 2025 report. A RAG pipeline built on a local LLaMA 3.2 model correctly extracted structured epitope data from a Mayaro virus paper — proof, even in 2025, that the underlying idea worked when the tooling cooperated.
