In Silico Pipeline for De Novo Discovery of Pan-Genotypic Nanobodies Targeting CHIKV E1/E2 Glycoprotein

Overview

This pipeline integrates evolutionary conservation analysis, structural modeling, generative AI, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations to design nanobodies (VHHs) that bind conserved, surface‑exposed epitopes on the Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) envelope glycoproteins. The workflow is divided into five modules, each with recommended open‑source tools and specific criteria.

Module 1: Epitope Mapping & Conservation Analysis

Objective: Identify hyper‑conserved, surface‑exposed residues on CHIKV E1/E2 that are suitable for broad‑neutralizing antibody binding.

1.1 Sequence Collection

  • Databases:
  • NCBI Virus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/virus/) – filter by Chikungunya virus, protein = E1 or E2.
  • VIPR (https://www.viprbrc.org/) – curated CHIKV sequences with metadata.
  • GISAID (https://www.gisaid.org/) – for recent outbreak strains.
  • Inclusion criteria:
  • Full‑length E1 or E2 sequences from all three genotypes: East/Central/South African (ECSA), Asian, and West African.
  • Remove duplicates and sequences with >5% ambiguous residues.
  • Aim for ≥100 sequences per genotype to capture diversity.

1.2 Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA)

  • Tool: MAFFT v7 (https://mafft.cbrc.jp/alignment/software/) with the --auto option for large datasets.
  • Alternative: Clustal Omega (http://www.clustal.org/omega/) for very large alignments.
  • Post‑processing: Trim poorly aligned columns using TrimAl (-gt 0.8 to keep columns with >80% occupancy).

1.3 Conservation Scoring

  • Tool: ConSurf (https://consurf.tau.ac.il/) – calculates residue‑specific conservation scores using an empirical Bayesian method.
  • Input: MSA of E1/E2 sequences.
  • Output: Normalized conservation scores (1–9, where 9 = most conserved).
  • Alternative: Rate4Site (https://www.tau.ac.il/~itaymay/cp/rate4site.html) for evolutionary rate estimation.

1.4 Surface Exposure Filtering

  • Structural template: High‑resolution cryo‑EM structure of the CHIKV E1‑E2 trimer (e.g., PDB 6NK5, 6NK6, or 7KLV).
  • Tool: PyMOL (https://pymol.org/) or UCSF ChimeraX (https://www.cgl.ucsf.edu/chimerax/).
  • Procedure:
    1. Map conservation scores onto the structure using the color_by_conservation script.
    2. Calculate solvent‑accessible surface area (SASA) per residue with get_area or DSSP.
    3. Define “hyper‑conserved surface‑exposed” residues as those with:
    • ConSurf score ≥ 8 (highly conserved)
    • Relative SASA ≥ 30% (exposed to solvent)
    • Located in regions known to be involved in receptor binding or fusion (e.g., E2 domain B, E1 fusion loop).

1.5 Epitope Clustering

  • Group contiguous conserved surface residues into epitope patches using a distance cutoff of ≤ 4 Å (Cα‑Cα).
  • Prioritize patches that:
  • Span ≥ 15 residues (sufficient for nanobody binding).
  • Are present in the pre‑fusion conformation (see Module 2).
  • Show no known glycosylation sites (check NetNGlyc 1.0).

Output: A list of candidate epitope patches with residue numbers, conservation scores, and SASA values.

Module 2: Structural Target Preparation

Objective: Prepare high‑quality structural models of the CHIKV trimer in the biologically relevant conformation for downstream docking and simulations.

2.1 Template Selection

  • Database: RCSB PDB (https://www.rcsb.org/).
  • Preferred templates:
  • Pre‑fusion state: PDB 6NK5 (CHIKV E1‑E2 trimer, 3.3 Å), 6NK6, or 7KLV (higher resolution).
  • Criteria:
    • Resolution ≤ 4.0 Å.
    • Contains the full E1‑E2 ectodomain.
    • Represents the pre‑fusion, mature virion conformation (neutralizing antibodies typically target this state).
  • Conformational state verification:
  • Compare with known post‑fusion structures (e.g., PDB 3N40) to confirm the pre‑fusion arrangement of E1 domain II and the fusion loop.
  • Use the E1‑E2 angle and the position of the β‑ribbon connector as diagnostic features.

2.2 Model Refinement and Relaxation

  • Tool: Rosetta (https://www.rosettacommons.org/) – relax application with the beta_nov15 score function.
  • Procedure:
    1. Clean the PDB file: remove water, ligands, and non‑protein atoms.
    2. Add missing loops/termini using Rosetta loopmodel or MODELLER (https://salilab.org/modeller/).
    3. Perform constrained relaxation:
    • relax.linuxgccrelease -s input.pdb -use_input_sc -constrain_relax_to_start_coords -ignore_unrecognized_res -nstruct 10
      4. Select the lowest‑energy model with the smallest Cα RMSD to the starting structure.
  • Glycosylation handling:
  • If glycans are present in the template, retain them as they may influence epitope accessibility. Use the Glycan‑Relax protocol in Rosetta or CHARMM‑GUI (https://www.charmm-gui.org/) to model glycosylated residues.

2.3 Protonation State Assignment

  • Tool: PROPKA (https://github.com/jensengroup/propka) via PDB2PQR (https://server.poissonboltzmann.org/).
  • Assign protonation states at pH 7.4 (physiological).
  • Save the prepared structure in PDB format for docking.

Output: A relaxed, protonated PDB file of the CHIKV E1‑E2 trimer in the pre‑fusion conformation, with identified conserved epitope patches highlighted.

Module 3: Binder Generation (Generative AI)

Objective: Generate nanobody sequences that adopt the canonical VHH fold and target the conserved epitope defined in Module 1.

3.1 Scaffold Selection

  • Canonical VHH structure: Use a high‑resolution nanobody structure (e.g., PDB 4KRP, 5VNV) as the scaffold.
  • Key features to preserve:
  • Immunoglobulin fold with a single variable domain.
  • Conserved framework residues (FR1‑FR4) that maintain stability.
  • Extended CDR3 loop (typical length 12–18 residues) for deep pocket binding.

3.2 Backbone Generation with RFdiffusion

  • Tool: RFdiffusion (https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion).
  • Input:
  • Target protein structure (CHIKV trimer) with the epitope patch defined as a “binder target” using the contigmap or target specification.
  • Nanobody scaffold constraints: specify the VHH fold using a “binder” contig that enforces the framework region geometry.
  • Command example:
    bash python scripts/run_inference.py \ inference.input_pdb=chikv_trimer.pdb \ inference.output_prefix=output/nb \ 'contigmap.contigs=[A1-150/0 30-50]' \ inference.num_designs=1000 \ diffuser.partial_T=50 \ denoiser.noise_scale=1.0
    (Adjust contig to match the VHH length and CDR3 length range.)
  • Post‑generation filtering:
  • Keep designs with pLDDT > 80 (predicted local distance difference test).
  • Cluster by backbone RMSD (< 2 Å) to remove duplicates.

3.3 Sequence Design with ProteinMPNN

  • Tool: ProteinMPNN (https://github.com/dauparas/ProteinMPNN).
  • Procedure:
    1. For each RFdiffusion backbone, run ProteinMPNN to design sequences that stabilize the fold and the interface.
    2. Use the --ca_only flag if only Cα traces are available.
    3. Constrain the CDR loops to favor amino acid compositions typical of nanobodies (e.g., high Tyr, Ser, Gly content) by providing a bias matrix.
    4. Generate 5–10 sequences per backbone.
  • Filtering:
  • Retain sequences with ProteinMPNN score < 0.5 (lower is better).
  • Check for the presence of the hallmark VHH tetrad (Phe42, Glu44, Arg45, Trp52 – IMGT numbering) to ensure proper folding.
  • Remove sequences with unpaired cysteines (unless a conserved disulfide is present).

3.4 Structure Prediction and Validation

  • Tool: AlphaFold2 (https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold) or ColabFold (https://github.com/sokrypton/ColabFold).
  • Predict the 3D structure of each candidate nanobody (monomer) and the nanobody‑antigen complex.
  • Quality checks:
  • pLDDT > 80 for framework regions.
  • Predicted aligned error (PAE) < 5 Å at the interface.
  • RMSD to the designed backbone < 1.5 Å.

Output: A set of 100–500 candidate nanobody sequences with predicted structures, ready for docking.

Module 4: In Silico Binding Affinity & Cross‑Reactivity Screening

Objective: Rank candidates by predicted binding free energy and stability across multiple CHIKV genotype variants.

4.1 Molecular Docking

4.1.1 Rigid‑Body Docking (Fast Pre‑Screen)

  • Tool: ZDOCK (https://zdock.umassmed.edu/) or ClusPro (https://cluspro.bu.edu/).
  • Procedure:
  • Dock each nanobody to the conserved epitope region of the CHIKV trimer.
  • Use blocking residues to restrict the search to the epitope patch.
  • Select the top 10 poses per nanobody based on Z‑score or cluster size.
  • Filter: Keep complexes with interface RMSD < 5 Å from the designed binding mode.

4.1.2 Flexible Docking and Refinement

  • Tool: HADDOCK 2.4 (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/haddock2.4/) or RosettaDock (Rosetta docking_protocol).
  • Input:
  • Ambiguous interaction restraints (AIRs) derived from the conserved epitope residues.
  • Allow side‑chain flexibility at the interface.
  • HADDOCK parameters:
  • run.cns with flexible_interface segments.
  • Water refinement step to improve interface packing.
  • Output: HADDOCK score (weighted sum of van der Waals, electrostatic, desolvation, and restraint energies).
  • RosettaDock alternative:
  • Use docking_protocol with docking_local_refine to sample local perturbations.
  • Score with interface_energy (I_sc) and binding_energy (ddG).

4.1.3 Binding Free Energy Estimation

  • Tool: PRODIGY (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/prodigy/) or Rosetta ddG_monomer.
  • Calculate ΔG (kcal/mol) for each refined complex.
  • Threshold: ΔG ≤ −10 kcal/mol for promising binders.

4.2 Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations

Objective: Assess the stability of the nanobody‑antigen interface and the effect of genotype‑specific mutations.

4.2.1 System Preparation

  • Tool: CHARMM‑GUI (https://www.charmm-gui.org/) Solution Builder or GROMACS pdb2gmx.
  • Force field: CHARMM36m (for proteins) with TIP3P water.
  • Box: Cubic box with ≥ 12 Å padding, neutralized with 0.15 M NaCl.
  • Genotype variants:
  • Build three separate systems using representative E1/E2 sequences from ECSA, Asian, and West African genotypes.
  • Introduce genotype‑specific mutations into the template structure using PyMOL or MODELLER, followed by a short energy minimization.

4.2.2 Simulation Protocol (GROMACS)

  1. Energy minimization: Steepest descent, 5000 steps.
  2. Equilibration:
    - NVT (100 ps, 310 K, V‑rescale thermostat).
    - NPT (100 ps, 1 bar, Parrinello‑Rahman barostat) with position restraints on heavy atoms.
  3. Production: 200 ns (minimum) to 500 ns, NPT ensemble, 2 fs time step, LINCS constraints.
  4. Replicates: Run 3 independent simulations per complex to assess convergence.

4.2.3 Analysis

  • Stability metrics:
  • RMSD of the nanobody and the epitope region over time (stable if RMSD < 3 Å after equilibration).
  • Interface hydrogen bond occupancy (>60% occupancy for key contacts).
  • Binding free energy via MM/PBSA or MM/GBSA (gmx_MMPBSA tool).
  • Cross‑reactivity assessment:
  • Compare ΔG_bind across the three genotype systems.
  • A candidate is considered cross‑reactive if ΔG_bind varies by < 2 kcal/mol across genotypes and the interface RMSD remains < 2.5 Å.

Output: For each candidate, a report containing docking scores, MD‑derived ΔG_bind, interface stability metrics, and cross‑reactivity profile.

Module 5: Selection Criteria for Lead Candidates

Objective: Rank candidates using a multi‑parameter scoring matrix to select 5–10 lead nanobodies for experimental validation.

5.1 Scoring Matrix

Criterion Weight Scoring Method Ideal Value
Binding Energy (ΔG) 30% Average MM/PBSA ΔG across genotypes ≤ −15 kcal/mol
Epitope Conservation Score 25% Mean ConSurf score of contacted epitope residues (≥ 8) 9
Cross‑Reactivity Breadth 20% Standard deviation of ΔG across genotypes (lower = better) σ < 1 kcal/mol
Interface Stability 15% RMSD of interface Cα after 200 ns MD (average across genotypes) < 2.0 Å
Developability 10% Aggregation propensity (CamSol, Solubis) and hydrophobicity (GRAVY) CamSol > 0.5, GRAVY < −0.5

5.2 Developability Assessment

  • Tools:
  • CamSol (http://www-mvsoftware.ch.cam.ac.uk/index.php/camsol) – predicts solubility from sequence.
  • Solubis (http://solubis.switchlab.org/) – identifies aggregation‑prone regions.
  • Aggrescan3D (http://biocomp.chem.uw.edu.pl/A3D/) – structure‑based aggregation prediction.
  • Criteria:
  • No strong aggregation hot‑spots in CDRs.
  • Predicted solubility score above the threshold for well‑behaved VHHs.

5.3 Final Ranking

  • Normalize each criterion to a 0–1 scale (1 = best).
  • Compute weighted sum:
    [
    \text{Score} = 0.30 \cdot S_{\Delta G} + 0.25 \cdot S_{\text{cons}} + 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{cross}} + 0.15 \cdot S_{\text{stab}} + 0.10 \cdot S_{\text{dev}}
    ]
  • Select top 5–10 candidates for experimental testing.

Output: Ranked list of lead nanobody candidates with detailed scores, ready for gene synthesis and in vitro characterization.
